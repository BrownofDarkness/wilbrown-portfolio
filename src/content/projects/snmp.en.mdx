During my time at IBAASS, I designed and operated an end-to-end observability pipeline to collect, transform, and index SNMP metrics from roughly **1000 multi-vendor network devices** (Cisco IOS, Cisco ASAv, Juniper, Fortinet). The challenge: turn a raw stream of SNMP OIDs into ECS-compliant data usable in Kibana, with a **scalable, normalized, and highly available** architecture.

## Context

The fleet to monitor spanned four families of network devices over SNMP v2c/v3. Zabbix already handled collection but the data stayed trapped inside Zabbix itself — no cross-device correlation, no usable long-term history. The goal: export these metrics into Elasticsearch in a **standard ECS format** so we could cross them with application logs, build rich Kibana dashboards, and hit a 99.99% uptime SLA on the monitoring chain itself (because a system that monitors everything else has to be more reliable than everything else).

## Architecture

I designed a **7-layer architecture with high availability** at every tier:

**Layer 1 — SNMP Collection**  
Two parallel **Zabbix proxies**, polling via SNMP v2c/v3 across the fleet. Vendor-specialized templates. Polling frequency from 30s to 5min depending on metric criticality.

**Layer 2 — Processing**  
Two **Zabbix servers in Active/Standby HA** (native Zabbix 7.x HA) with shared **PostgreSQL** database. Failover detected in < 30s, transparent on the proxy side.

**Layer 3 — HA Distribution**  
**HAProxy + Keepalived** with failover VIP (MASTER/BACKUP). Round-robin 50/50 distribution to the Kafka connectors layer. VIP failover in < 3s.

**Layer 4 — Kafka Connectors**  
Two HTTP→Kafka connectors in **ACTIVE/ACTIVE** mode, receiving Zabbix exports as NDJSON and publishing them to the primary topic.

**Layer 5 — Streaming and classification**  
**Kafka 4.0 cluster in KRaft mode** (no ZooKeeper). **Kafka Streams** (Java) classifies metrics by vendor in real time and routes them to dedicated topics (`cisco-ios`, `cisco-asav`, `juniper`, `fortinet`). Replication factor 2.

**Layer 6 — ECS Transformation**  
Two **Logstash nodes in Active/Active**, running **4 specialized pipelines** (one per vendor). Full **ECS 8.11.0** normalization of fields (`system.cpu.*`, `network.interface.*`, etc.). Automatic load balancing through Kafka consumer groups.

**Layer 7 — Storage and indexing**  
**3-node Elasticsearch cluster** in Docker with per-vendor data streams. Naming convention: `metrics-<vendor>.<templateid>.<metricCategory>_snmp-default`. **ILM policies**: Hot 3 days / Warm 30 days / Cold long-term archive.

## Outcomes

- **~1000 devices in production** monitored via SNMP (Cisco IOS, Cisco ASAv, Juniper, Fortinet)
- **23M+ documents indexed**, ~1M docs/day growth, 21GB+ of data stored
- **End-to-end latency < 3 minutes P95** (SLA target: < 5 min)
- **Zero SPOF across 6 of the 7 layers**: Active/Standby Zabbix, HAProxy MASTER/BACKUP, Kafka KRaft, Logstash A/A, 3-node ES cluster
- **4 vendor-specialized Logstash pipelines** with full **ECS 8.11.0** normalization
- **Kafka Streams** for automatic classification: a single primary topic routes to vendor-specific topics with no human intervention
- **Kafka 4.0 KRaft cluster**: modern ZooKeeper-less mode, fewer moving parts to operate
- **ILM policies** Hot/Warm/Cold for automatic index lifecycle management
- **Runbook documentation** for every service: start, restart, troubleshooting, failover scenarios

<Callout type="info" title="Why not Telegraf or Prometheus?">
Telegraf SNMP works well but doesn't handle inter-system buffering or multi-vendor classification as gracefully as Kafka + Kafka Streams. Prometheus is pull-based and doesn't match the existing Zabbix model. Keeping Zabbix as the primary collector and routing it to Elastic via Kafka was the compromise that respected legacy while unlocking the new analytical use cases.
</Callout>

## Lessons

- **Kafka KRaft changes the game.** No more ZooKeeper to manage cluster quorum — fewer moving parts, simpler configuration, same durability guarantees. If you're starting a Kafka cluster today, KRaft is the right default.
- **When you monitor everything else, you have to be more reliable than everything else.** HA at every layer (Active/Standby Zabbix, HAProxy+Keepalived, Logstash A/A, ES cluster) isn't gold-plating. It's what separates a monitoring system you respect from one you work around with manual scripts at the first incident.
- **Multi-vendor SNMP: the MIBs are the real challenge.** Cisco IOS, ASAv, Juniper, Fortinet expose their metrics differently. The MIB → ECS mapping work was 70% of the time on this project. Once the mapping is done, the Kibana dashboards become uniform regardless of vendor — big long-term payoff.
